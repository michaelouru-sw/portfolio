National E-Learning Platform

Ministry of Education, Eswatini & UNICEF · 2023 · Moodle / Learning Passport

View LMS Launch Sample Module

The Problem

Eswatini's Ministry of Education, with UNICEF, needed to move national curriculum delivery onto a digital platform that could reach students with widely varying literacy levels and inconsistent or no internet connectivity — without leaving low-resource schools behind. The country needed a single, scalable LMS carrying 600+ courses, not a series of disconnected pilots.

Target Audience

Primary and secondary school students nationwide, spanning a wide range of literacy levels and device/connectivity access, plus the 10,000+ educators who needed to be trained to deliver and manage content on the new platform.

My Role

Project lead overseeing LMS architecture, instructional design, and gamification strategy, directing a distributed team of LMS architects, instructional designers, multimedia developers, gamification experts, and accessibility specialists, plus running educator training and stakeholder workshops with the Ministry and UNICEF.

Design Process & Strategies

Given the scale — 600+ courses across multiple grade levels — I used a SAM (Successive Approximation Model) approach: rapid prototyping of course templates, quick stakeholder review cycles, and iterative refinement, rather than a single long upfront design phase. Every course was built mobile-first with offline access options to work around inconsistent connectivity, and I layered in gamification mechanics (progress badges, point systems) to sustain engagement for students with lower baseline literacy. Adaptive learning techniques adjusted content difficulty to individual learner pace. A monitoring & evaluation framework and pilot testing phase preceded national rollout.

The Solution

A mobile-first, offline-capable national LMS built on the Learning Passport platform with 600+ gamified, adaptive courses and a built-in monitoring dashboard for the Ministry.
